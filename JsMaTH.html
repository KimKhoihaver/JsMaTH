Decoding Js(n): When Transfinite Mathematics Reaches the Limits of Human Thought

November 7, 2026 Googology & Set Theory

Introduction

Googology is the study of extraordinarily large numbers. Familiar examples such as a Googol, \(10^{100}\), and even Graham's number are enormous by everyday standards. Yet they can be dwarfed by numbers described through powerful mathematical frameworks involving formal logic, Set Theory, and Large Cardinals.

Instead of relying only on repeated exponentiation or elaborate numerical notation, these approaches use the expressive power of mathematical languages and the statements that can be proved within an axiom system. The proposed function \(J_s(n)\) explores this idea: how large a natural number can be characterized when the description is restricted by a finite symbol budget?

The Main Equation

The expression below is the proposed definition at the center of this article.

Proposed formal definition
$$ J_s(n)=\max \left\{x \in \mathbb{N} \mid \exists \varphi \in \mathcal{L}_{\omega_1, \text{Berkeley}}^{\infty}\left(\operatorname{Len}(\varphi) \leq n \wedge \mathrm{ZFC}^{-}+\mathrm{BC} \vdash \psi(x, \varphi)\right)\right\} $$

1. Breaking Down the Formula

Each part of the expression has a specific role in describing the numbers that qualify.

  • \(J_s(n)\): The largest natural number \(x\) uniquely definable by a formula \(\varphi\) containing at most \(n\) symbols, relative to the stated formal framework.
  • \(\mathcal{L}_{\omega_1, \text{Berkeley}}^{\infty}\): An infinitary formal language augmented with symbols intended to refer to a Berkeley Cardinal.
  • \(\operatorname{Len}(\varphi) \leq n\): The symbol-length constraint. It limits the size of each description so that the definition cannot use an unlimited expression.
  • \(\mathrm{ZFC}^{-} + \mathrm{BC} \vdash \psi(x, \varphi)\): The proposed axiomatic foundation. \(\mathrm{ZFC}^{-}\) denotes a version of Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory without the Axiom of Choice, while \(\mathrm{BC}\) denotes the Berkeley Cardinal axiom. The proof predicate \(\psi\) is intended to express that \(\varphi\) uniquely defines \(x\) in this theory.
Core takeaway

A stronger formal language can describe vastly larger numbers within the same symbol budget. The expressive strength of the language and the axioms matters just as much as the length of the description.

A note on mathematical precision. This equation is a proposed schematic definition. To make \(J_s(n)\) a fully well-defined function, \(\psi(x,\varphi)\) must be specified precisely as a unique-definability condition, and the set of eligible values must be nonempty and bounded so that its maximum exists. The infinitary language and the formal meaning of \(\mathrm{BC}\) also need exact definitions.
